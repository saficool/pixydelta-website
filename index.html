The moment that matters

At the end of 12 weeks, don't just ask, “What did you learn?”

Ask them, “What did you build?”

And let them show you — a game they designed, a Python program they wrote, or an AI-assisted project they can explain in their own words.

AI is already part of their world

The goal isn't to make children depend on AI. It's to help them understand it.

Children are growing up with AI tools around them. At PixyDelta, we introduce AI as something to question, test, and create with. They learn that AI can be useful, but it can also be wrong — so their own thinking still matters.

This part of the program comes from my own hands-on experience: I've spent about 5 years building AI applications. I use that practical background to choose approachable examples, explain what is happening behind the tool, and keep the focus on understanding rather than hype.

Think before trusting

Learn to question outputs instead of accepting every AI answer.

Use AI to create

Explore ideas and projects while keeping the child in control of the thinking.

Use it responsibly

Discuss sensible boundaries, mistakes, and why human judgment matters.

Explain what they made
